## Ingredients

- 8 ripe baby bananas (about 450 g peeled), spotted and soft
- 1 cup (120 g) cassava flour, plus 2–4 tbsp more if the dough feels wet
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar, if the bananas need it
- About 1/2 cup (120 ml) vegetable oil, for shallow frying

## Instructions

1. **Mash the bananas:** Peel the baby bananas and mash them in a bowl until mostly smooth. A few small lumps are fine. Stir in the salt. Taste the mash. If it is not sweet enough, stir in the sugar.
2. **Make the dough:** Add the cassava flour a little at a time and mix with your hand until a soft dough forms. It should hold a ball and leave your palm mostly clean, and it should still feel tender. If it smears, add a spoonful more flour. If it cracks and feels stiff, it has too much flour.
3. **Shape:** Pinch off pieces and flatten them into rounds about 2 inches (5 cm) wide and 1/2 inch (1 cm) thick. You should get about 12.
4. **Fry:** Heat the oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. It is ready when a small pinch of dough sizzles at once. Fry in batches, without crowding, until both sides are deep gold, about **2–3 minutes per side**. The center should be cooked through, not raw and pale.
5. **Serve:** Lift them onto a rack or a paper-lined plate. Eat warm, as they are.

## Bree's Notes

Kabalagala is what you make when the baby bananas have gone past eating out of hand and nobody wants to waste them. The sweetness is in the fruit, so wait until they are really ripe. If they are only just yellow, mash in 1 teaspoon of sugar.

Cassava flour is what gives them that slight chew. Add it slowly. Too much, and they fry up hard.

They are best the day they are fried, with tea. Leftovers keep in a tin overnight and warm through in a dry pan.
