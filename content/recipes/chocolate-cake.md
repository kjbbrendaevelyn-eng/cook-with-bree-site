## Ingredients

### Cake

- 1 3/4 cups (220 g) all-purpose flour
- 3/4 cup (75 g) unsweetened cocoa powder
- 2 cups (400 g) granulated sugar
- 1 1/2 tsp baking powder
- 1 1/2 tsp baking soda
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- 1 cup (240 ml) buttermilk, room temperature
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) vegetable oil
- 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1 cup (240 ml) hot coffee (or hot water)

### Chocolate frosting

- 1 cup (225 g) unsalted butter, softened
- 3/4 cup (75 g) unsweetened cocoa powder
- 3 1/2 cups (420 g) powdered sugar, sifted
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) heavy cream or milk, plus more as needed
- 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

## Instructions

1. **Prep:** Preheat oven to **350°F (175°C)**. Grease and flour two **8-inch** or **9-inch** round cake pans (or line the bottoms with parchment).
2. **Dry ingredients:** In a large bowl, whisk flour, cocoa powder, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt until no cocoa lumps remain.
3. **Wet ingredients:** In another bowl, whisk eggs, buttermilk, oil, and vanilla until smooth.
4. **Combine:** Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and whisk until just combined. Slowly pour in the hot coffee and whisk until the batter is smooth and thin — it will look more like chocolate milk than cake batter. That's what you want.
5. **Bake:** Divide the batter evenly between the pans. Bake **28–32 minutes**, until a toothpick inserted in the centre comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Cool in the pans **10 minutes**, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely.
6. **Frosting:** Beat butter until creamy. Sift in the cocoa powder and mix until smooth. Add powdered sugar one cup at a time, alternating with the cream. Beat in vanilla and salt. Whip on medium-high until light and fluffy, **2–3 minutes**. Add a splash more cream if it's too thick.
7. **Assemble:** Place one cake layer on a plate. Spread a generous layer of frosting. Top with the second layer and frost the top and sides. Smooth with a spatula, or swirl for that homemade bakery look. Chill briefly before slicing if the kitchen is warm.

## Bree's Notes

This is the chocolate cake I make when someone needs comfort that looks like a celebration. The buttermilk keeps it soft, the oil means it never dries out, and the hot coffee is the secret — you won't taste coffee, but it deepens the chocolate so it tastes expensive. Use a good cocoa powder; Dutch-process makes it darker and smoother, natural cocoa works too. Don't panic when the batter looks thin — that's how you get a tender crumb. The layers freeze well wrapped tightly — frost when thawed. For cupcakes, bake about **18–20 minutes**. And if you want to go all out, sandwich a thin layer of chocolate ganache or raspberry jam between the layers before the frosting.
