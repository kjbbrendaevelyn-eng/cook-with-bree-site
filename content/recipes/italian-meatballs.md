## Ingredients

### Meatballs

- 2 slices (about 60 g) soft white bread, crusts removed and torn
- 12 oz (340 g) ground beef, 80% lean
- 8 oz (225 g) ground pork
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 cup (45 g) finely grated Parmesan, plus more for serving
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 tbsp finely chopped flat-leaf parsley
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- Pinch of freshly grated nutmeg

### Sauce

- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 small yellow onion, finely chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 can (28 oz / 800 g) whole peeled tomatoes
- 1 tsp Italian herb mix
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp sugar, only if the tomatoes taste sharp
- 6 fresh basil leaves, torn
- Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional)

### To serve

- 12 oz (340 g) spaghetti
- Extra Parmesan and parsley

## Instructions

1. **Tear the bread:** Put the torn bread in a large bowl and mash it with a fork so it breaks into soft crumbs.
2. **Mix:** Add the beef, pork, egg, Parmesan, garlic, parsley, salt, pepper, and nutmeg. Mix with your hands until the bread disappears and the meat just holds together. Stop there. A hard mix makes a dense meatball.
3. **Shape:** Wet your hands and roll about 16 balls, roughly 1 1/2 inches (4 cm). Set them on a plate.
4. **Brown:** Heat the olive oil in a wide Dutch oven or deep skillet over medium heat. Brown the meatballs in two batches, turning until a few sides are colored, about **6–8 minutes**. They finish in the sauce. Transfer to a plate.
5. **Start the sauce:** In the same pot, cook the onion with a pinch of salt until soft, about **5 minutes**. Add the garlic and cook **30 seconds**. Pour in the tomatoes and crush them with a spoon. Stir in the Italian herb mix, salt, red pepper flakes, and the sugar if you are using it. Simmer **5 minutes**.
6. **Simmer:** Nestle the meatballs back in and spoon sauce over them. Cover and simmer gently **20 minutes**, until the center of a meatball reaches 165°F (74°C). Stir in the basil.
7. **Serve:** While the meatballs simmer, cook the spaghetti in well-salted water until al dente. Toss it with a ladle of sauce, then top with meatballs, more sauce, Parmesan, and parsley.

## Bree's Notes

This is the pot I make when the day needs to feel like someone cooked for you. Torn soft bread is what keeps the middle tender, the way a dry meatball never is. Pork is there so they stay juicy. All beef works if that is what you have; just skip extra-lean.

Don't pack the balls tight. A squeeze makes them bouncy instead of tender. If the sauce looks thin, uncover for the last 5 minutes. If it looks thick, loosen it with a splash of pasta water.

They freeze well. Cool the meatballs in their sauce, then freeze in a container for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight and reheat gently with a splash of water. For a sandwich, skip the pasta and tuck two meatballs into a roll with sauce and a little mozzarella.
