## Ingredients

- 3 cups cooked beans (or 2 cans, drained and rinsed — kidney, pinto, or mixed)
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tomatoes, chopped (or 1 cup canned crushed tomatoes)
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 green bell pepper, diced (optional)
- 1 carrot, diced (optional)
- 1 tsp curry powder
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp paprika
- 1/4 tsp cayenne or chili flakes (optional)
- 2 cups water or vegetable stock
- Salt and black pepper, to taste
- Fresh cilantro or parsley, for serving

## Instructions

1. **Sauté the base:** Heat oil in a medium pot over medium heat. Add onion and cook until soft and deep golden, about **8–10 minutes**.
2. Stir in garlic and cook for **1 minute** until fragrant. Add tomato paste and cook another minute.
3. Add chopped tomatoes, curry powder, cumin, paprika, and cayenne (if using). Cook until the tomatoes break down into a thick sauce, about **5–7 minutes**.
4. Stir in bell pepper and carrot (if using) and cook for **2–3 minutes**.
5. **Add the beans:** Stir in the beans. Pour in water or stock and season with salt and pepper.
6. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer uncovered for **25–30 minutes**, until the stew is thick and the flavors have come together. Stir occasionally so nothing sticks.
7. **Taste and finish:** Adjust salt and pepper. For a creamier gravy, mash a spoonful of beans against the side of the pot and stir them back in.
8. **Serve:** Ladle over rice, next to matooke, or with warm chapati. Top with fresh herbs.

## Bree's Notes

Bean stew is pure home cooking — the kind of pot that quietly feeds everyone without asking for attention. Canned beans make it a weeknight win; dried beans you cooked ahead taste even better. Don't rush the onions — that golden base is the whole gravy. Leftovers thicken overnight and are excellent the next day with a fried egg on top.
