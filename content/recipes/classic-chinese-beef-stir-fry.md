## Ingredients

### For the beef

- 1 lb (450 g) flank steak or sirloin, thinly sliced against the grain
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp cornstarch
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1/2 tsp sugar
- 1/4 tsp black pepper

### For the sauce

- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce (optional, for colour)
- 1 tbsp cornstarch
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) water or beef stock
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 tsp sesame oil

### For the stir fry

- 3 tbsp vegetable oil, divided
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1 medium onion, sliced
- 1 red bell pepper, sliced
- 2 cups broccoli florets
- 2 green onions, sliced, for serving
- Cooked rice or noodles, for serving

## Instructions

1. **Slice and velvet the beef:** Freeze the steak for **15 minutes** so it's easier to slice thin. Cut against the grain into thin strips. Toss with soy sauce, cornstarch, sesame oil, sugar, and black pepper. Set aside **10–15 minutes** while you prep everything else.
2. **Mix the sauce:** In a small bowl, whisk soy sauce, oyster sauce, dark soy (if using), cornstarch, water, sugar, and sesame oil until smooth. Set aside.
3. **Prep the vegetables:** Have the garlic, ginger, onion, bell pepper, and broccoli ready — stir fries move fast once the pan is hot.
4. **Cook the beef:** Heat **2 tbsp** oil in a large skillet or wok over high heat until shimmering. Add the beef in a single layer (work in two batches if needed). Stir-fry until browned but still a little pink in the middle, about **2–3 minutes**. Transfer to a plate.
5. **Cook the vegetables:** Add the remaining **1 tbsp** oil. Stir-fry onion and broccoli for **2 minutes**. Add bell pepper, garlic, and ginger and cook **1–2 minutes** more, until the vegetables are bright and still crisp.
6. **Combine:** Return the beef and any juices to the pan. Give the sauce a quick stir (the cornstarch settles) and pour it in. Toss everything over high heat for **1–2 minutes**, until the sauce thickens and coats the beef and vegetables in a glossy glaze.
7. **Serve:** Spoon over hot rice or noodles and finish with green onions.

## Bree's Notes

This is my "I want takeout but I can do better" dinner. The cornstarch on the beef is the Chinese restaurant trick — it keeps the meat soft and velvety even over high heat. Slice against the grain, or the beef will chew like a rubber band. High heat and a hot pan matter: if the pan isn't hot enough, the beef steams instead of searing. Swap the broccoli for snow peas, mushrooms, or whatever's in the fridge. Leftovers reheat well for lunch the next day — just add a splash of water so the sauce loosens up again.
