## Ingredients

### Dough
- 4 cups (500 g) all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- 1/4 cup (50 g) sugar
- 2 1/4 tsp (1 packet) instant yeast
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 1/4 cups (300 ml) cold milk
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened

### Butter block
- 1 1/4 cups (280 g) cold unsalted butter

### Egg wash
- 1 egg, beaten with 1 tbsp milk

## Instructions

1. **Make the dough:** In a large bowl, mix flour, sugar, yeast, and salt. Add cold milk and softened butter. Mix until a shaggy dough forms, then knead briefly until it just comes together — don't overwork it. Shape into a rectangle, wrap, and refrigerate **1 hour**.
2. **Make the butter block:** Place cold butter between two sheets of parchment. Pound and roll into a roughly **6×8 inch** rectangle, about 1/2 inch thick. Keep cold — refrigerate if it softens.
3. **Lock in the butter:** Roll the chilled dough into a rectangle large enough to wrap the butter block (about **10×12 inches**). Place butter in the center, fold the dough over like an envelope, and pinch seams closed.
4. **First fold:** Roll into a long rectangle, about **8×20 inches**. Fold into thirds like a letter. Wrap and chill **30–45 minutes**.
5. **Second & third folds:** Repeat the roll-and-letter-fold two more times, chilling **30–45 minutes** between each. After the third fold, wrap and refrigerate **overnight** (or at least 4 hours).
6. **Shape:** On a lightly floured surface, roll dough into a large rectangle about **1/4 inch thick**. Cut into long triangles. Gently stretch each triangle and roll from the wide base toward the tip. Curve into a crescent and place on a lined baking sheet, tip tucked under.
7. **Proof:** Cover loosely and let rise at cool room temperature until puffy and jiggly, about **1 1/2–2 hours**. They should look noticeably swollen but not collapsed.
8. **Bake:** Preheat oven to **400°F (200°C)**. Brush with egg wash. Bake **15–20 minutes**, until deep golden brown. Cool on a rack at least **10 minutes** before eating.

## Bree's Notes

Croissants are a labor of love — cold butter and patience are the whole secret. If the butter starts melting into the dough while you roll, chill everything and come back. Don't skip the overnight rest; it develops flavor and makes shaping easier. Day-old croissants are excellent warmed in a low oven or turned into almond croissants with a swipe of frangipane.
