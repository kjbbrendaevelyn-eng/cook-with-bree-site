## Ingredients

- 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
- 1/4 cup (50 g) sugar
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) coconut milk, plus 1–2 tbsp more if the dough feels stiff
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil, for the dough
- Vegetable oil, enough to come about 2 inches (5 cm) up a deep pot, for frying

## Instructions

1. **Mix the dough:** Stir the flour, sugar, baking powder, cardamom, and salt together. Add the egg, coconut milk, and the 1 tbsp oil. Mix, then knead **3–4 minutes** into a soft, smooth dough. It should feel tender and slightly tacky, and it should hold a ball. If it cracks, add a spoonful more coconut milk. If it smears, dust in a little flour.
2. **Rest:** Cover and rest **15 minutes**. The dough relaxes and rolls without springing back.
3. **Shape:** Roll the dough to about 1/2 inch (1 cm) thick. Cut triangles about 3 inches (8 cm) on each side. You should get about 12. Gather the scraps, re-roll once, and cut the rest.
4. **Fry:** Heat the oil to 350°F (175°C). A small pinch of dough should sizzle and rise slowly. Fry in batches, without crowding, turning once, until both sides are deep gold and the center is cooked through, about **2–3 minutes per side**. They should puff. If they brown in under a minute, the oil is too hot and the middle stays raw.
5. **Serve:** Lift them onto a rack. Eat warm, with tea.

## Bree's Notes

Mandazi should be soft in the middle, with a thin golden shell. The coconut milk is what keeps them tender. Cardamom is the spice I reach for. A pinch of nutmeg works in its place.

Keep the oil at a steady medium heat. Very hot oil colors the outside before the dough cooks. Crowding drops the temperature, and then they drink oil.

They are best the day they are fried, with tea. Leftovers keep in a tin overnight and warm through in a dry pan or a 300°F (150°C) oven. They also freeze well and thaw on the counter.
