## Ingredients

- 1 cup (150 g) raisins
- 1 cup (225 g) unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup (200 g) packed light brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg (optional)
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 3 cups (270 g) old-fashioned rolled oats (not instant)

## Instructions

1. **Plump the raisins:** Put the raisins in a small bowl and cover with hot water. Let them soak **10 minutes**, then drain and pat dry with a paper towel. This keeps them juicy instead of chewy little stones.
2. **Cream the butter and sugars:** In a large bowl, beat butter, brown sugar, and granulated sugar with a mixer (or a wooden spoon and some muscle) until light and fluffy, about **2–3 minutes**.
3. **Add eggs and vanilla:** Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla, scraping down the bowl as you go.
4. **Mix the dry ingredients:** In another bowl, whisk flour, baking soda, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt. Add to the butter mixture and mix on low just until no flour streaks remain.
5. **Fold in oats and raisins:** Stir in the oats and drained raisins with a spatula until evenly mixed. The dough will be thick.
6. **Chill:** Cover and refrigerate the dough for at least **30 minutes**. This stops the cookies spreading too thin and deepens the flavour.
7. **Prep to bake:** Preheat oven to **350°F (175°C)**. Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
8. **Scoop:** Roll heaped tablespoons of dough (about **2 tbsp** each) into balls and place them **2 inches** apart on the sheets. Press each one down slightly with your palm.
9. **Bake:** Bake one sheet at a time for **10–12 minutes**, until the edges are golden but the centres still look a little soft. They firm up as they cool.
10. **Cool:** Let the cookies rest on the sheet for **5 minutes**, then move them to a wire rack to cool completely.

## Bree's Notes

Oatmeal raisin cookies were the ones that always disappeared first from the tin, even though everyone claims they prefer chocolate chip. The trick is pulling them out while the middles still look underdone, because that's what keeps them soft and chewy for days. Soaking the raisins is a small step that makes a big difference. If you're not a raisin person, swap in chopped dates, dried cranberries, or a handful of chocolate chips, and nobody will complain. Store them in an airtight tin with a slice of bread, and they stay soft all week. Serve with a cup of tea.
