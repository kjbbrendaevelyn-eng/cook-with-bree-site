My parents never fought around us.

That was the rule of the house, spoken or not. Disagreements happened somewhere out of sight — behind a closed door, in a tone we weren't meant to catch. **Salma** and I grew up believing marriage could be warm without being loud, that love meant protecting the children from the sharp edges. For a long time, I thought that was the whole story.

Then I remember the fight that broke the pattern.

It got so heated that my mum locked the door to their room and told my dad to sleep on the couch. The house went still in that particular way houses do when adults have said something they can't unsay. Salma and I knew enough to stay quiet. We knew enough to understand that something rare was happening — not because our parents didn't love each other, but because the love had nowhere soft to land for a moment.

My dad said he couldn't sleep without her.

I believe him. Not as a line. As a fact. He had spent years choosing her in small ways — Friday stories, Valentine's fuss, the hand in the driveway — and a locked door was not going to be the end of that sentence.

So he climbed through the ceiling to get to her.

I still picture it when I need to remember what stubborn tenderness looks like: a grown man refusing the couch, finding the ridiculous route back to the person he loved, choosing closeness over pride. Mum had locked him out. He found another way in. Not to win the argument. To be near her.

They loved each other like that.

Not the postcard version — the real one. The kind that can get loud, get locked out, and still climb back. The kind that makes two daughters learn, without anyone saying it out loud, that passion and patience can live in the same house. That a fight doesn't erase a marriage. That sometimes love looks like refusing to sleep on the wrong side of a door.

I think about that night whenever someone tells me love should always be calm. Calm is beautiful. So is climbing through the ceiling.
